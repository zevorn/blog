## Preface

Since 2021, I have been working on architecture simulation and dynamic binary translation. Because of my job, I often need to debug various operating systems such as Linux, VxWorks, FreeRTOS, RT-Thread, SylixOS, AcoreOS, and ReWorks. This gave me a broad understanding of operating-system principles and real-world application scenarios. As embedded systems have become more complex and larger in scale, with multiple systems synchronizing and interacting, higher performance requirements have also been placed on digital embedded systems.

In the first half of 2024, I led the preliminary R&D of a high-performance JIT engine for full-system simulation. During that project, perhaps because I was sensitive to simulator performance, I became very interested in hardware virtualization technology. I learned that it is part of the operating-system kernel (Type-2).

Some time ago, while studying virtualization technology, I implemented a [lightweight KVM virtual machine][1] in C on my Youlan notebook, with about 200 lines of code, and shared it in the Lanshe group chat. After seeing it, Professor Guo Ke recommended the "Open Source Operating Systems Bootcamp" launched by Professor Chen Yu and Professor Xiang Yong from Tsinghua University.

> PS: A bit of history with Professor Chen Yu:
>
> In the winter of 2002, a group of operating-system enthusiasts were discussing technology online and wanted to study operating-system principles without being constrained by hardware. They proposed the idea of simulating hardware in software, which included the initiator of SkyEye — Professor Chen Yu of Tsinghua University.
>
> At that time, there were already similar projects internationally, such as ARMulator, developed by the uCLinux community, which could emulate the AT91 development board and support UClinux running on it. Based on the design framework and experience of ARMulator, Professor Chen Yu and others officially launched the SkyEye open-source project in 2003, and it has been widely used by hobbyists and universities at home and abroad.
>
> Since 2022, I have initiated and organized the renewed maintenance of the SkyEye open-source project, which is now open on the [Gitee platform][2].

## Bootcamp Launch

The 2024 Autumn/Winter Open Source Operating Systems Bootcamp launch ceremony (2024-09-29) introduced the bootcamp's background, goals, course content, and scale. The bootcamp received full support from the laboratory, covered more than 520 universities and 240 companies, and included Rust fundamentals, Q&A and algorithms, and hands-on operating-system projects. Its goal was to cultivate talent interested in open-source operating systems.

Below are the parts of the meeting that interested me most:

**Opening remarks (Chen Yu)**

- Opening and introduction: Chen Yu welcomed all participants and introduced the important guests, including Professor Chen Anqun from Peking University, Director Yang Bo from the Quansheng Laboratory, General Manager Yang Chun from vivo, and Director Wang Yanguang from the Open Source Foundation's Education and Training Department.

- Background and development: Chen Yu mentioned that the bootcamp began in 2020, jointly launched by him and a professor from Hong Kong, with the goal of sparking more students' interest in operating systems. The bootcamp joined hands with Pengcheng Lab to promote operating-system development and introduced new concepts such as one-page programming and componentized operating systems.

- Participation scale: The bootcamp attracted more than 3,000 participants and covered 524 universities, not limited to students but also including engineers.

- Course content: The bootcamp will cover Rust programming, OS-course experimental design similar to that used in Tsinghua University's computer science undergraduate curriculum, the design and implementation ideas of componentized operating systems, and practical projects such as microkernel virtualization.

- Importance and challenges of operating systems: Chen Yu emphasized the importance of operating systems as the key layer connecting hardware and applications, especially when dealing with modern operating systems with millions of lines of code (such as Linux), which need to solve real-time performance and security issues. He also mentioned that operating systems need to adapt to emerging fields such as smart cars and robots.

- Future trends: He pointed out that with the rapid development of AI, operating systems must make corresponding adjustments to support new application scenarios and improve security. At the same time, China's development in the new-energy-vehicle field also requires more research and innovation at the operating-system layer.

Finally, Professor Chen Yu said he looked forward to participants gaining knowledge, building friendships, and improving their abilities through this bootcamp, and previewed that experts from different fields would follow with deeper sharing.

**Exploring vivo's self-developed operating system on Rust (Yang Chun)**

- Opening thanks: Yang Chun expressed respect to the teachers and students attending the meeting and thanked Professor Chen Yu for the invitation, which gave him the opportunity to participate in this open-source operating-system bootcamp.

- vivo's investment in operating systems: Yang Chun mentioned that vivo has accumulated several years of operating-system R&D, and that at last year's developer conference it publicly unveiled its self-developed operating system for the first time. The system focuses on three core characteristics: intelligence, smoothness, and security.

- Why Rust was chosen: When developing its own operating system, vivo chose Rust mainly because of Rust's advantages in memory safety, such as the ownership model and lifetime management. These are crucial for building a secure operating system.

- Cooperation with academia: Yang Chun described how vivo connected with Professor Chen Yu's team. Although the two sides are in industry and academia respectively, they share a common goal of exploring the next generation of operating systems using Rust, so they quickly reached an agreement.

- Talent status in the industry: He believes that people who truly master Rust are still scarce in the industry, especially because the scale of Rust use in operating systems is still small. Therefore, vivo hopes to validate whether its technical path is correct through exchanges with the industry.

- Expectations for future cooperation: Yang Chun expressed support for the open-source operating-system bootcamp, believing that such activities help mature and develop the Rust ecosystem, and hoping that they will attract more talent to Rust and operating-systems work.

- Closing remarks: Finally, Yang Chun wished the event a complete success and thanked everyone for listening.

In summary, Yang Chun's talk focused on vivo's efforts in operating-system R&D, especially its decision to adopt Rust and Rust's security advantages, while emphasizing the importance of cooperation with academia and expectations for future talent development.

**Introducing the ArceOS development plan (Shi Lei)**

- Project background: The project aims to address the problems and challenges in existing operating systems, and sets R&D goals around security, high concurrency, real-time behavior, modularity, ecosystem compatibility, and customization, building a new componentized operating-system kernel on that basis.

- Project characteristics:
1. Multi-mode support: in addition to traditional monolithic kernels, it also supports virtualization infrastructure.
11. Componentized design: different kernel modes are built by composing components from a repository.
12. Based on Rust: it takes advantage of Rust's memory-safety and concurrency-safety strengths and explores kernel development based on Rust.

- Current progress: After two or three years of work, a basic component repository has been established, covering functional components at different layers and usable for building kernels in various modes. Current work is focused on the following directions:
1. Core component repository: supports constructing kernels in multiple modes.
7. Extensible monolithic kernel: compatible with the Linux API and able to run native Linux applications.
8. Virtualization: supports typical types of virtualization infrastructure.

- Future directions: The next priority is to continue developing the monolithic kernel, virtualization technology, and a unified async-coroutine-based framework and driver set. The plan is to lead participants through related project practice in phase four.
- Distribution development: Based on the above research, distributions for different application scenarios will be further developed.
- More information: Participants who want more details can check the past work records in the discussion area.

Professor Chen Yu added that this project was initially started by a Tsinghua University doctoral student as part of a PhD topic, and many teachers and students are now involved in extending it. A notable feature of the project is that it can be composed into customized configurations with different architectures and characteristics, which is considered one of the future directions of operating systems.
